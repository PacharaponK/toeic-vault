## Conversation 1 — Quarterly Budget Review

### Script

[W]: David, do you have a moment? I wanted to go over the Q2 budget figures with you before the meeting this afternoon.

[M]: Of course, Sarah. I was actually just reviewing them. It looks like we're about 15 percent over on the IT infrastructure costs.

[W]: Yes, I noticed that too. The server upgrade ended up being more expensive than we initially projected.

[M]: Right. We need to offset that somehow. Have you thought about scaling back the marketing campaign budget?

[W]: I have, but our marketing director won't be pleased. The campaign is already running.

[M]: I understand. What if we reduce the budget for the trade show next month instead? We could send fewer representatives.

[W]: That might work. How much would we need to cut to balance things out?

[M]: Around $8,000 should cover it. I can draft a revised budget proposal and send it to you before noon.

[W]: Perfect. Let's schedule a quick call with the marketing director after the meeting to explain the situation.

[M]: Agreed. I'll prepare all the figures and have everything ready for you by 11 a.m.

## Conversation 2 — Interview Scheduling

### Script

[M]: Linda, have you had a chance to review the applications for the senior analyst position?

[W]: Yes, I went through all twelve applications yesterday. I've narrowed it down to three strong candidates.

[M]: That's great. When were you thinking of scheduling the interviews?

[W]: I'd like to start next week if possible. Would Tuesday afternoon work for you?

[M]: Tuesday is difficult — I have a client presentation in the morning that might run long. Could we do Wednesday instead?

[W]: Wednesday works for me. Should we do back-to-back interviews or spread them out?

[M]: Let's space them out — maybe 45 minutes each with a 15-minute break in between.

[W]: Good idea. I'll send calendar invitations to the candidates and copy you on the emails.

[M]: Also, could you prepare a brief profile for each candidate beforehand? It would help to review their backgrounds.

[W]: Absolutely. I'll have the profiles ready by Monday morning so we both have time to look them over before Wednesday.

## Conversation 3 — Client Shipment Complaint

### Script

[W]: Mr. Harrison, I just got off the phone with Greenfield Industries. They're very upset about the shipment delay.

[M]: How serious is it? This is the second time this month.

[W]: Very serious. Their project manager said if the issue isn't resolved by Friday, they'll reconsider their contract with us.

[M]: That's alarming. What's the current status of the shipment?

[W]: It's stuck at the regional distribution center. Apparently there was a documentation error in the customs forms.

[M]: Okay. Contact the logistics team immediately and have them correct the paperwork. That should clear things up within 24 hours.

[W]: I'll do that right away. Should I offer Greenfield a discount as compensation?

[M]: Yes — offer them a ten percent discount on their next order. I'll draft an apology letter to send to their director.

[W]: Alright. I'll also call their project manager back to let them know we're actively resolving the situation.

[M]: Good. Keep me updated every two hours until the shipment is confirmed to be on its way.
